# Pressure test: Michigan's +7.5% Democratic governor vote gain, 2022 → 2026

**2026 results are UNOFFICIAL / uncertified (as of Nov 17, 2026).** In 27 counties, the 2026 county D/R split is an NBC News county count scaled to the SOS statewide remainder (* in the chart and CSV). **Wayne and Oakland are among them**, so their D/R split is an estimate. Their 2026 ballot totals are SOS turnout figures. Ottawa uses the county canvass (‡). Muskegon, Ogemaw and Wexford have known data-quality problems (†). The 2024 Harris and Trump counts are SOS county results.

## Bottom line
The +7.5% gain is real in the county files, but it is **concentrated, not broad-based**. Democratic raw votes rose in only 45 of 83 counties and fell in 38. **Wayne alone accounts for 38% of the statewide net gain, and Wayne plus Kent for 50%.** Ten counties account for 93%. Outside Wayne and Oakland, the Democratic and Republican gains are almost identical: D +95,810 (+6.0%) and R +95,689 (+6.2%). No county's 2026 Democratic vote reached its 2024 Harris vote.

## 1–2. Size of the gain and where it came from
- **Statewide:** 2,430,505 → 2,613,240, a net gain of **+182,735 (+7.52%)**. Gross gains were +190,688 and gross losses −7,953. Shares of the net gain sum to 100% only after counting the 38 negative counties. Each county's share of gross gains is also in the CSV.
- **County % change:** median +0.6%, mean +1.4%, SD 5.7, range −10.9% (Sanilac) to +15.2% (Wayne).
- **Top 10 counties by raw gain:**

| County | D gain | % of net gain | % of 2022 D votes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wayne* | +69,519 | 38.0 | 18.8 |
| Kent | +22,237 | 12.2 | 6.7 |
| Oakland* | +17,406 | 9.5 | 15.8 |
| Washtenaw* | +15,022 | 8.2 | 5.6 |
| Macomb | +14,048 | 7.7 | 8.2 |
| Ingham | +9,686 | 5.3 | 3.4 |
| Ottawa‡ | +7,323 | 4.0 | 2.4 |
| Kalamazoo | +6,416 | 3.5 | 3.0 |
| Berrien | +4,023 | 2.2 | 1.2 |
| Genesee | +3,784 | 2.1 | 4.1 |

- **Concentration:**
  - The top 10 counties produced 92.7% of the net gain (88.9% of gross gains). They hold 69.3% of 2022 Democratic votes and 63.0% of 2026 ballots.
  - Counties needed to reach a share of the net gain: **2 for 50%, 6 for 80%, 19 for 100%**. The running total peaks at 104%, and the 38 losing counties pull it back to 100%.
- **Is the gain proportional to size?** No. Larger counties gained disproportionately: Spearman correlation of % gain with population is +0.52 (p < 0.001).
  - **Over-contributors:** Wayne (38.0% of the gain vs 18.8% of 2022 D votes), Kent (12.2% vs 6.7%), Washtenaw, Ingham and Ottawa.
  - **Under-contributors:** Oakland (9.5% vs 15.8%) and Genesee (2.1% vs 4.1%).
  - **Most of the rural Northern Lower Peninsula and the Thumb** lost Democratic votes. Examples: St. Clair −1,078, Bay −765, Lapeer −735, Sanilac −648 (−10.9%).

## 3. Ceiling check against 2024
- **2026 Democratic vote vs 2024 Harris vote:** 0 counties reached it. The 2026/Harris ratio had a median of 0.936, with a range of 0.883 (Berrien) to 0.992 (Macomb). Statewide it was 0.955.
- **2026 Republican vote vs 2024 Trump vote:** 0 counties reached it. The 2026/Trump ratio had a median of 0.747, with a range of 0.671 (Wayne) to 0.855 (Leelanau). Statewide it was 0.743.
- **Ballots 2026/2024:** median 0.825, range 0.772–0.912. Statewide it was 0.849.
- **Democrats held up better than Republicans everywhere.** The D ratio exceeded the ballot ratio by an average of +0.107 (SD 0.028), and the R ratio fell short by −0.081 (SD 0.026). That is the usual midterm pattern: the president's party (Trump's Republicans) drops off more.
- **Counties more than 2 SD above the mean D residual:**
  - Branch: z = 2.69; D/Harris 0.980 vs ballots 0.799.
  - Otsego*: z = 2.31.
  - Macomb: z = 2.19; D/Harris 0.992 vs ballots 0.825.

  None is a data-quality county. These look like modest outliers in Democratic retention, not gain spikes: Branch's Democratic vote rose only +2.1% over 2022, and Macomb's +7.1%, with Republicans up +10.6%. With 83 counties, about two would land above 2 SD by chance alone.
- **Counties at or above 2 SD on the R residual:** Otsego* (z = 2.00).

## 4–5. Wayne and Oakland
- **Wayne*:** D +69,519 (+15.2%) and R +13,269 (+7.4%), with ballots +95,491 (+14.7%, SOS).
  - 38.0% of the statewide net D gain and 10.0% of the statewide R gain.
  - 15.4% of 2026 ballots and 14.5% of 2022 ballots.
  - Wayne's ballot growth from SOS turnout counts is real and independent of the NBC split. Most of its Democratic gain matches its turnout jump.
- **Oakland*:** D +17,406 (+4.5%) and R +23,842 (+10.0%), with ballots +7.0%.
  - 9.5% of the D gain and 18.0% of the R gain.
  - 14.0% of 2026 ballots.
  - Oakland moved **toward Republicans** in raw votes.
- **Together:** 47.6% of the net D gain and 28.0% of the R gain, against 29.5% of 2026 ballots and 34.6% of 2022 Democratic votes. They drove just under half the Democratic gain, almost all of it from Wayne.
- **Without Wayne:** the statewide Democratic gain drops to +5.7% and the Republican gain to +6.7%.

## 6. Did Republicans grow in the counties where Democrats grew?
- **Both parties grew in 40 of the 45** counties where Democrats rose.
- **D % growth outpaced R % growth** in 23 of those 45 (21 by raw votes). The median gap (D % − R %) was +0.11 pts, essentially even: median D +4.6%, median R +5.9%.
- **Across all 83 counties,** D % growth beat R % growth in only 23, with a median gap of −4.75 pts.
- **Ties to turnout:** D % change and R % change each correlate about equally with ballot % change (r = 0.58 and 0.56).
- **Totals:** in the 45 rising counties, Democrats gained 190,688 and Republicans 107,724. Almost all of the gap comes from Wayne (+56,250 D minus R) and Kent (+18,996).

## What it means
The gain isn't a uniform statewide lift. It's mostly a **Wayne County turnout surge** plus Democratic-trending suburban and college areas: Kent, Washtenaw, Ingham, Ottawa, Kalamazoo and Berrien. Large parts of rural Michigan moved the other way. That pattern fits higher turnout among Democratic-leaning voters in a few places and candidate-specific shifts, and every county stayed below its 2024 presidential totals. It doesn't fit a flat add-on across counties. The data can't show *why* Wayne surged. Because Wayne's D/R split is an NBC-scaled estimate, its 38% figure should be rechecked when the certified county canvass is posted.

## Caveats
- **Unofficial 2026 results:** all 2026 numbers are uncertified.
- **NBC-scaled estimates:** the 27 NBC-scaled counties produced 57.5% of the net D gain, so the concentration findings lean on estimated splits for Wayne, Oakland and Washtenaw.
- **Data-quality counties:** Muskegon† (D +3,074, rank 11) shows more governor votes than ballots in 2026. Ogemaw† has an incomplete 2026 count. Wexford† has a ballot/vote mismatch.
- **Not a fraud test:** this measures where the gain came from and whether it looks unusual. It can't prove or rule out fraud.
